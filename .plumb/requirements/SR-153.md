---
id: SR-153
title: Repeated worker bookkeeping avoids redundant file access
statement: Plumb reduces repeated log-head reads and replacements, log lock churn, and unchanged progress
  writes while preserving durable hash-chained entries, worker authority, fresh events between jobs, and
  timely progress and completion reporting.
examples:
- id: EX-1
  given: a valid log head and a job with multiple verdicts
  when: the worker persists its verdicts
  then: every verdict remains a separate valid chained entry and bounded batches reduce head and lock
    operations
- id: EX-2
  given: a batch interrupted after some entries have been persisted
  when: a subsequent append runs
  then: it recovers the persisted head and extends the actual chain without trusting an older pointer
- id: EX-3
  given: repeated identical running progress updates
  when: updates occur within the heartbeat interval
  then: redundant writes are coalesced while changed counts and final or failed states are written immediately
- id: EX-4
  given: multiple backlog jobs in one worker pass
  when: earlier jobs persist verdicts
  then: later jobs observe those events and final progress and cards reflect the complete pass
