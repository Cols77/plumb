title: Understand intent and text with language models, not fixed rules
quote: My intent is that most of the time the system shouldn't rely on deterministic tooling to understand
  intent or process text, unless we're sure the deterministic solution can handle any type of situation.
readback: 'Wherever plumb has to understand what you mean (your intent, your answers, your corrections,
  the meaning of a record), it uses a language model, not fixed rules such as keyword lists, regular expressions
  or sentence splitting. Fixed rules stay only where they are provably complete for every case: hashes,
  file paths, record IDs, whether a message was logged, whether a record was shown.'
will:
- Use a language model for every step that interprets your words or a record's meaning.
- Keep deterministic code for checks that are exact by nature (provenance, hashes, IDs, links, test results).
- Remove today's word-based rules (affirmation words, objection words, plain-words matching) as the model-based
  reading lands, with no fallback to them.
wont:
- Add more keyword or sentence-splitting rules to understand your messages.
assumptions:
- A model's reading is logged with your exact words and can be checked and undone, so it is never a hidden
  decision.
- When no model is reachable, plumb shows an error and confirms nothing; it does not fall back to rules.
